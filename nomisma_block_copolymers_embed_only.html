Formulation FAQs

Frequently asked questions

What is PLGA–PEG–PLGA triblock copolymer?

It is an A–B–A polymer architecture with PLGA terminal blocks and a PEG middle block. Selected compositions can exhibit thermoresponsive gelation in water, but behavior depends on block lengths, lactide:glycolide ratio, concentration and test conditions.

Is PLGA–PEG–PLGA the same as PEG–PLGA–PEG?

No. The order of blocks defines different polymer architectures and can change self-assembly and sol–gel properties. Confirm the exact chemical architecture and chain-end functionality before selecting a grade.

What is mPEG–PLGA diblock copolymer used for?

mPEG–PLGA is used in formulation research involving polymeric micelles, nanoparticles, poorly soluble APIs, drug encapsulation and controlled-release systems.

Which properties should a formulator specify?

Typical inputs include target molecular weights of both blocks, lactide:glycolide ratio, dispersity, PEG end-group, expected application, drug loading target and required polymer quantity.

How should gelation at 37 °C be evaluated?

Assess sol–gel transition experimentally at the intended polymer concentration, vehicle, pH and ionic strength. Gel formation at 37 °C should not be assumed for every PLGA–PEG–PLGA composition.
